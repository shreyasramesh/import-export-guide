# How to Make Your Own Physical Product — and Export It: A Practical Guide

A single-page, static website that presents a generic guide to making a physical product from zero, and then to exporting it — taking an Indian D2C brand global.

## Contents

### Part I — Make the product

- **Part 1 — Key terms and landed cost:** MOQ, tooling, certification, payment terms, tariffs, a worked landed-cost example ($8 EXW → about $12.50 landed), and the rule that retail price should be 3–5× landed cost.
- **Part 2 — Find and vet a factory:** direct listings, sourcing agents, and trade shows, plus a vetting checklist.
- **Part 3 — Samples to mass production:** design files, timelines (production 30–45 days), the golden sample, and third-party inspection.
- **Part 4 — Negotiation:** seven rules for working with factories without damaging product quality.
- **Part 5 — Cash flow and protection:** export credit insurance terms, action against counterfeiters, and a second warehouse near the factory.
- **The playbook:** eight steps for starting a hardware product from zero.
- **Landed-cost calculator:** pre-filled with the guide's worked example; recomputes landed cost and the 3–5× pricing verdict as numbers change.
- **Glossary:** 23 searchable key terms.

### Part II — Export: taking an Indian D2C brand global

- **Export 1 — The right to win:** price competitiveness, best-in-class quality, and intrinsic "Made in India" categories (Ayurveda, yoga, traditional wellness).
- **Export 2 — Build for the world:** no need to win India first; market-making is expensive; "global is not a country."
- **Export 3 — Category notes:** Ayurveda, leather goods, jewelry (the largest shipped category), home decor, apparel cautions, and occasion wear via trunk shows.
- **Export 4 — Country ranking:** US first, then UK, UAE, Australia; Canada last; Saudi Arabia and continental Europe caveats.
- **Export 5 — Research tools:** Jungle Scout for finding what sells and which sellers are India-based.
- **Export 6 — Compliance:** claims discipline and per-SKU costs (US about $10/$100, UK about $20–30, UAE about $15); remove MRP and fix labels.
- **Export 7 — Go-to-market sequence:** marketplace first (25–40% commission), D2C after roughly 100+ reviews per SKU, then offline — and the recent shift that makes D2C-first viable.
- **Export 8 — Launch process and budget:** about 3 months to launch; about $100,000 for a serious 10-product launch, or a lean start at about $10,000 ($3,000 fixed, $7,000 advertising).
- **Export 9 — Fulfilment models:** dropship from India (returns are refunded and destroyed) versus FBA (dead-inventory risk).
- **Export 10 — Unit economics:** a ₹100 domestic product must sell at ₹250–300 in the US; worked cost stacks for dropship (~30% margin), FBA (~30%), and an enablement partner (~25%, with a ₹50,000/month minimum ad commitment).
- **Export 11 — Advertising expectations:** about 10% of revenue; early ROAS 2–3×; no profit for 6–12 months; the kill benchmark is ROAS 4–5× by months 6–12.

### Shared

- **Research agent:** a visitor supplies their own Google AI API key and a research topic. The key is kept only in the browser tab's session storage and is sent only to the Gemini API. The agent answers from the full text of both parts of the guide plus live web search, separates guide guidance from researched facts, cites sources, and ends with next steps.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser, or serve the folder with any static file host — no build step and no server code are required. The research agent is the only feature that needs a network call, and it runs entirely from the visitor's browser using the visitor's own API key.
