Overview

Why physical is different

Hard to cloneMoat

Software can be copied in a weekend. A physical product requires molds, component specs, and supplier trust — months of work and capital.

Device + AppEdge

Pairing hardware with firmware, subscriptions, and data beats either alone. Software builders hold an edge because hardware teams are usually weak at software.

North star

Cash flow, not unit price, is the real measure of a great production lead. Check every decision against it.

Key terms and landed cost

Nine fundamentals before you request a single quote.

MOQVolume
100s – 1,000s

Hundreds for OEM/white-label; thousands for custom. Not a law — just where the factory stops losing money.

Unit priceTiers
~10% spread

Small vs. large batch savings top out around 10%. Don't anchor on 5,000-unit pricing when you can afford 500.

Tooling / NREUpfront
$3K – $6K+

Simple steel mold; complex ones start in the tens of thousands. Paid upfront before unit #1 exists.

CertificationGates timeline
FCC / CE

Required for electronics. Shortcut: use an already-certified radio module to avoid redoing full certification.

Samples & AQLQuality
$50 – $200+

Rarely credited back. Write your AQL (acceptable quality limit) into the contract before production begins.

IncotermsQuotes
EXW · FOB · DDP

EXW = factory door. FOB = loaded on ship. DDP = delivered, duties paid. Always ask which basis a quote uses.

Payment termsCash flow
30 / 40 / 30

Standard is 30/70; never pay 100% upfront. Push for 30% deposit, 40% after inspection, 30% at loading/port.

Tariffs20–37.5%+
Check HTS

China goods carry layered duties and de minimis is gone. Routing via Vietnam/Thailand to dodge duty is illegal transshipment.

Pricing ruleTarget
3–5× landed

Covers ads, returns, and retail margins. An $8 EXW item (~$12.50 landed) must retail for roughly $48–$60.

Holiday traps & buffers

Chinese New Year shuts factories for weeks (a January order is already late). Golden Week (first week of October) slows Q4 runs. Contractual delay penalties rarely stick — build schedule buffers instead.

Part 4

Seven rules for factory negotiation

Relationships (guanxi) matter, but value decides. Click any rule to expand details.

01
Negotiate setup cost, not MOQSolve the factory's break-even problem instead of asking for 300 units.
First orders barely break even for a factory. Offer a setup fee, a slightly higher unit price for a smaller run, their standard color, an existing mold, or one SKU instead of five.
02
Sell the second orderFrame order #1 as a trial and show your launch & reorder roadmap.
Factories rank buyers and research your public track record (LinkedIn, social presence, press). A credible public footprint unlocks much better treatment than an empty profile.
03
Don't squeeze the unit priceFactory margins are only 10–20% — squeezing 10% quietly ruins quality.
If you crush their margin, they'll still say yes — then swap in cheaper plastic, shorter screws, and smaller batteries. Legitimate lever: source packaging or materials yourself from a lower-cost province and ship them to the factory (~10% EXW savings).
04
Talk to the boss, not the sales repUse WeChat directly — reps carry ~1% commission and zero decision power.
Sales reps are a communication tax between you and the owner. Factories actually run on WeChat, not email.
05
Give facePush back privately one-to-one; never embarrass anyone in front of their team.
Respect is operational, not decorative, in long-term manufacturing partnerships.
06
Be the customer factories wantEasy customers jump the queue when lines are full.
Pay on time, send crisp specs, and never change designs after the mold is cut. That earns better prices and terms than threatening to walk away.
07
Protect IP, molds & supply chainBilingual NNN agreement, China trademark, mold ownership, clean origin.
Put mold ownership and exclusivity in writing. Sign a Chinese/English NNN agreement before sharing designs (US NDAs are weak in China). File a China trademark within 6 months of your US filing (first-to-file rules). Verify materials have no Xinjiang-linked inputs (cotton, aluminum, polysilicon) to avoid US Customs holds.
Part 5

Cash-flow and protection plays

SinosureNet 60–120

China's state export credit insurer. The factory insures your invoice so you can pay later — sell inventory before paying for it.

  • Early win: smaller deposit + 60–90d terms; best case net-120.
  • Factory applies; expects ~2 years of your financials.
  • Factories using it signal export maturity.
Schedule AAnti-clone

US federal court action listing dozens of counterfeit sellers on one schedule — far faster than piecemeal takedowns.

  • TRO freezes marketplace listings & seller payouts.
  • Requires registered US IP (trademark / design patent) pre-launch.
  • Use a specialist firm — judges now scrutinize bonds & joinder.
China 3PLDual 3PL

Add a second warehouse next to your factory alongside your US 3PL.

  • Ship rest-of-world straight from China without stocking every country.
  • Covers US stockouts so ads never pause.
  • Full tariffs apply to US parcels — use as backup + international lever.
Playbook

Start from zero in 8 steps

  1. 1
    Don't start custom

    Modify an existing factory product (logo, packaging, tweak). Zero molds, lower MOQ.

  2. 2
    Pre-sell first

    Validate demand with pre-orders or crowdfunding before buying inventory.

  3. 3
    Check 3–5× landed math

    If retail price can't clear 3–5× landed cost, change the product.

  4. 4
    Sample every contender

    Samples benchmark real quality and expose overcharging fast.

  5. 5
    Meet bosses in person

    Fly to China. Trust runs both ways when both sides put up capital.

  6. 6
    Trademark US & China

    File early in both jurisdictions to block squatters and enable Schedule A.

  7. 7
    Scale the moat later

    Only after validation: buy custom molds and negotiate Sinosure terms.

  8. 8
    Keep one working file

    Track factory checklist, questions, and landed-cost sheet across every run.

Export 1

The right to win

To take an Indian D2C brand global, you need at least one of three structural edges.

01 Price & HandworkLow MOQ

China wins on mass scale; India wins on embroidery, hand-finishing, and profitable small-batch customization.

02 Best-in-Class QualityCraft

Categories where Indian manufacturing already leads globally — leather goods stand out for durability and finish.

03 "Made in India"Origin moat

Like Swiss chocolate or French wine, origin itself signals quality: Ayurveda, yoga, and traditional wellness.

Export 2

Build for the world

Skip "Win India first"

Physical products can be built in India for foreign markets from day one — just like SaaS. (Caution: if it's failing in India, fix the product first.)

Match local demand

US buyers pull ashwagandha (health & vitality); Indian demand skews to shilajit. Never assume identical positioning.

Don't market-make

Educating a foreign market from scratch drains capital and rarely works for young brands. Ride existing demand.

"Global is not a country"

You cannot launch five countries at once. Pick one geography with proven demand and win it first.

Export 3

Category notes

Ayurveda & wellnessTop pick

Complete right to win with strong existing demand abroad — ashwagandha in the US is the clearest case.

Jewelry & accessoriesLargest volume

Biggest shipped category. Thrives on eBay & Etsy via photos + reviews — no brand or market-making needed.

Leather goodsGifting

Position as gifting (journals, small goods); wins on durability and craft.

Home decorLight only

Throws, sheets, cushion covers, light wood+metal pieces. Avoid heavy pure wood (shipping kills margin). Western design helps.

ApparelHigh return risk

Sizing & natural dye variance trigger brutal returns (destroyed abroad). Stick to free-size items like scarves, shawls, and stoles.

Occasion wearTrunk shows

Influencer-hosted hotel pop-ups in NY, LA, London & Dubai with on-spot 24–48h tailoring sell out in volume.

Export 7

Go-to-market sequence

1. Marketplace25–40% fee

Buys predictability, zero upfront CAC guesswork, early volume, and customer reviews.

2. Own D2C Site100+ reviews

Once SKUs pass ~100 reviews, buyers trust your direct site and you stop paying marketplace commissions.

3. Offline RetailScale

With marketplace + D2C humming, pitch larger-format distributors instead of small boutiques.

Recent shift (~last 3 months)

Marketplaces have tightened compliance and delisted even compliant foreign sellers. Meanwhile Meta/TikTok CAC dropped and 40K–100K influencers cost <$1,000/mo (down from ~$10K). Still try marketplace first — go D2C first if blocked.

Export 8

Launch process (~3 months) & budget

6-step launch checklist

  • Pick one geography deliberately.
  • Compare carriers (DHL, FedEx, enablers): $100+ express lands in 3–4 days; standard cross-border takes 6–8 days.
  • Engage a compliance partner directly or via an enablement platform.
  • List on marketplaces (Amazon Global Selling / eBay). Shortcut: latch existing Amazon India listings onto amazon.com to carry over reviews.
  • Build A+ content — hire a marketplace content agency (D2C copy isn't enough).
  • Run ads — marketplace + Meta ads (pointing to your store while building reviews).
Serious LaunchRecommended

Reference

Glossary

23 terms

MOQ

Minimum order quantity. Hundreds of units for OEM/white label; thousands for custom. Marks where the factory stops losing money.

OEM

An existing factory product sold with your logo and packaging (white label). The low-MOQ, no-mold starting point.

NRE / Tooling

Upfront mold and setup cost ($3K–$6K for a simple steel mold; tens of thousands for complex ones). Paid before unit #1 exists.

FCC

US electronics certification required to sell legally. Using a pre-certified radio module can avoid redoing full certification.

CE

European counterpart to FCC approval, required to sell electronics legally in Europe.

AQL

Acceptable quality limit. Contracted standard defining acceptable vs. defective units — the reference for factory inspections.

EXW

Ex Works. Price at the factory door; freight, customs, and duty all sit with the buyer.

FOB

Free On Board. Factory gets goods onto the ship; ocean freight and duty after that sit with the buyer.

DDP

Delivered Duty Paid. All-in price to your door with duties paid — easiest basis to compare when offered.

HTS code

Harmonized Tariff Schedule classification that sets a product's duty rate. Always confirm before trusting landed math.

Transshipment

Routing goods through a third country (e.g., Vietnam or Thailand) to disguise origin and dodge duty. Illegal.

DFM

Design for manufacturing. Factory review of your CAD files flagging what's expensive or impossible before cutting steel.

T1 sample

First units shot from a new mold. Never right the first time — plan fix-and-repeat iterations.

Golden sample

One approved unit, signed and kept by both buyer and factory as the quality reference for production.

Guanxi

Relationships and trust in Chinese business culture. Matters, but value still decides.

NNN agreement

Non-use, non-disclosure, non-circumvention agreement in Chinese & English, enforceable in China (unlike a US NDA).

Sinosure

China's state export credit insurer. Factories insure your invoice so they can safely offer 60–120 day payment terms.

Schedule A case

US federal court action listing dozens of counterfeit sellers at once to freeze marketplace listings and payouts.

3PL

Third-party logistics warehouse. Pair a US 3PL with a China 3PL for international orders and stockout backup.

Landed cost

True per-unit COGS including EXW, loading, freight, duty, brokerage, and inspection. Retail should be 3–5× landed.

Canton Fair

Spring & fall trade show in Guangzhou with ~1,000 factories in one hall — one of the most efficient sourcing routes.

Qichacha

Chinese company-records platform used during factory vetting to inspect registration and court disputes.

WeChat

The messaging channel Chinese factories actually run on — reaches bosses far faster than email.
